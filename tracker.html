how to actually run this planner
4 quick moves a day, no cap. read once → run on autopilot.
1
morning — log it
open today's row (auto-numbered, don't stress the date). after every lecture/chapter, drop it straight in Topic/Content. → benefit: nothing slips through, zero "what did I even study" moments.
2
right after class — 5-min dump
class ends → close laptop → stare at a wall for 5 mins → rebuild it from memory. tap the 5m circle. → benefit: locks the lecture in before it fades, this is the highest-ROI 5 mins of your day.
3
before bed — 12hr lock-in
30-45 mins, zero notes, full active recall of today. tap 12h when done. → benefit: today's stuff officially moves from "shaky" to short-term memory.
4
every day — clear what's due
scan +1D → +30D. each circle = serial no. of an old lesson due today (it's pulling from your past days, that's the Ebbinghaus trick). gold=due, red=overdue, green=done. → benefit: this is the actual mechanism that beats the forgetting curve — skipping this step = skipping the whole point.
tl;dr → log in the morning, 5-min recall after every lecture, 12hr deep recall at night, clear whatever's due/overdue daily. the planner handles the scheduling math — you just show up.

Memory Strength — est., based on the forgetting curve
Forgetting Curve
How to read this: the curve is Hermann Ebbinghaus's classic retention model — recall starts at 100% right after you study a topic and decays exponentially the longer you go without revisiting it. Each gold spike marks a review you actually completed on time, which resets retention back up — exactly the "spaced repetition" effect the 5min/12h/Day1…Day30 schedule is built around. A red dashed stretch means a review was missed, so the curve keeps fading uninterrupted instead of bouncing back — that's the visual cost of skipping a scheduled revision. The steeper and longer the unbroken decay, the more of that topic is realistically slipping out of memory. This is a simplified approximation (exponential decay, τ≈6 days) for motivation and prioritization, not a clinical measure of your actual recall.
