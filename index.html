// Our story

Why WYNKO?

As a student, I kept running into the same wall: I'd sit for hours, finally feel like a topic had clicked — and a few days later, it was gone. Not "rusty." Gone, like I'd never opened the book.

That sent me down a rabbit hole, and that's where I found Hermann Ebbinghaus. Back in 1885, this German psychologist ran an experiment on himself — memorizing meaningless syllables and testing his own recall over days and weeks. What he found became one of psychology's most replicated results: the forgetting curve. Without any reinforcement, memory doesn't decay in a straight line — it falls off a cliff almost immediately, then keeps fading, fast at first and slower later. Within a day, a huge chunk of what felt "learned" is already slipping.

But Ebbinghaus found the fix too: if you revisit that information at the right moments — not too early, not too late — each review flattens the curve a little more, and the memory sticks for longer each time. That's spaced repetition, and it's why WYNKO's tracker is built around 8 specific intervals (5m, 12h, +1d, +2d, +4d, +7d, +15d, +30d) instead of just "revise sometime."

Once I understood that, I couldn't just keep it to myself. I built WYNKO so any student — not just me — could get the benefit of this without having to track 8 intervals across dozens of topics by hand on paper. It does the scheduling, you just show up and tick the box.
